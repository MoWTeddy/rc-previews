Common companies get common results for their owners.

You take all the risk. You carry the pressure, solve every problem, absorb all the stress. And the company still owns your time, occupies your head, and takes too much from your life - often while paying you last, or not at all.

That is not successful ownership. It is not why you started. But it is common ownership - and it is what most owners live with until they quit, or the business dies.

Growth often makes it worse. More people, more complexity, more decisions - a bigger company and an even smaller life. Leave it unchanged, and you stay overworked, under-rewarded and permanently on call.

It doesn't have to stay that way.
