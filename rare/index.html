Successful from the outside. Common underneath.

Plenty of companies look impressive - the team, the customers, the reputation. And underneath, they remain poor businesses for their owners. Sound familiar?

  • You are still the best salesperson the company has.
  • Important decisions wait until you are available.
  • The profit does not reflect the effort or the risk.
  • The team escalates problems rather than owning them.
  • The company could not run for a fortnight without you.
  • Its value depends on you staying exactly where you are.
  • You have revenue, but remarkably few genuine choices.

Most companies are common. That's why they're so sh*t for the owner. Yours doesn't have to be.

No. 1 · Your Circle

Eight owners. Three hours. One material decision.

Your Circle is a private group of six to eight established owners at a similar stage and scale - matched deliberately, never with a direct competitor - meeting in person, in your city, every month. It is chaired and supported by people who have built, grown and sold companies of their own.

This is not networking. The same owners return each month, know your numbers, and hold you to what you said you would do. Sessions run to a working agenda: progress against commitments, one material decision examined properly, and one member's business taken apart in depth. What is said in the room stays in the room.

House rules

How the room is kept worth being in.

  1. i.Nobody is here to sell to you

    No advisers hunting clients, no introducers, no pipelines. It is the first thing we protect.

  2. ii.What is said in the room stays in the room

    No notes, no quotes, no exceptions.

  3. iii.Experience outranks theory

    Every table is anchored by people who have done the thing - not studied it or posted about it.

  4. iv.Candour is part of the contract

    Difficult conversations are had, advice is challenged, and comfortable answers get questioned.

  5. v.Members contribute as well as take

    The room only works because everyone in it makes it work.

  6. vi.Membership is reviewed, not assumed

    Every year we ask whether it has earned its place. If it hasn't, we say so first.
